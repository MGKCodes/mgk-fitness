Roughly 5 to 30 reps produce comparable hypertrophy when weekly volume is
equated and sets are taken close to failure. **Strength is different:** heavier
loads carry a real advantage for strength specifically.

Proximity to failure interacts with load, and this is the part usually left
out. At low loads a set stopped several reps short leaves the highest-threshold
motor units largely unrecruited, because they are only called on once fatigue
demands them. At heavy loads those fibres are working from the first rep, so
stopping two or three short is still enough.

**What this means for programming.** "8-12 for size, 1-5 for strength" is
folklore with a grain of truth in the second half. For someone training for
size, the rep range is largely a practicality choice — what the movement suits,
what the equipment allows, how long the session can be — and getting close
enough to failure matters more than the number. For someone training for
strength, the load itself is the point, and lower reps are how you get it.

So sets and reps are a coaching judgement per movement, not a lookup from a
goal. A hinge and a lateral raise do not want the same prescription even in the
same session with the same goal.

**If this is wrong** — if a narrow range is genuinely superior for size — then
letting the coach choose per movement is worse than a fixed scheme, and the
prescription should go back to a table.
