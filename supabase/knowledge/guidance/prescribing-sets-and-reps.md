Sets and reps belong to the movement and the person, not to the goal alone.
Rough starting points:

- **Heavy compounds** — squat, hinge, bench, overhead press. 3-5 sets of 3-6 for
  someone training for strength; 3-4 sets of 6-10 for size. These are where
  fatigue is bought most expensively, so keep the set count sane.
- **Secondary compounds** — rows, pulldowns, incline work, lunges. 3-4 sets of
  8-12 under either goal.
- **Isolation** — curls, lateral raises, calves, extensions. 2-4 sets of 10-20.
  Higher reps here cost little and are easier to take close to failure safely.

Two adjustments worth making:

**Bias reps down for strength, not up for size.** Per c6, size is available
across a wide range, so there is no need to push reps up chasing it. Strength is
load-specific, so that is where the range genuinely narrows.

**A movement somebody is new to gets fewer sets and more reps**, until the
technique is reliable. A first attempt at a Romanian deadlift is a skill session
whatever the plan says.

Never prescribe a weight. The app works that out from what this person has
actually lifted, and where there is no history it asks them to pick something
they could do the top rep count with, comfortably.
