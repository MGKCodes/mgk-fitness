A split is a scheduling wrapper, not a philosophy. The question it answers is
"how do we fit this person's weekly volume into the days they have", and the
day count does most of the work.

Rough starting points, not rules:

- **2-3 days** — full body. Splitting the body up at this frequency trains each
  part once a week, which c3 says is a poor way to deliver its volume.
- **4-5 days** — upper/lower. Everything twice, sessions short enough that the
  last movement still gets attention.
- **6 days** — push/pull/legs twice through.

**None of these is required.** An upper/lower with a dedicated arm day, a
four-day plan that gives a lagging body part its own session, a hybrid — all
fine, provided every muscle trained at volume gets two exposures, nothing is
trained hard on back-to-back days, and the week's volume lands in range. That is
what gets checked; the name is yours.

Five does not divide into three, so a push/pull/legs rotation pinned to fixed
weekdays over five days trains legs once. Prefer upper/lower there, or an
arrangement that gives legs a second day.
